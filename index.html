Why a nanopore specific tool?

CRISPResso2 is the standard pipeline for CRISPR amplicon analysis and it is well built for what it was designed around: short read Illumina data. Its preprocessing reflects that, including Illumina adapter trimming, paired end merging, and an alignment step where the default minimum alignment score requires roughly 60% homology to the reference amplicon for a read to be retained. That threshold is documented, deliberate, and appropriate quality control for short read data.

Nanopore reads carry higher per base error. Our working hypothesis is that this pushes a larger share of nanopore reads below that threshold on default settings, so they are excluded before quantification. Where attrition is severe, the surviving coverage can be too thin to support a reliable estimate.

CRISPRpreserve uses alignment parameters tuned for nanopore error profiles. It classifies and reports every read rather than dropping reads that fail a homology cutoff, flagging low confidence alignments instead.

This is a domain fit argument, not a claim of superior accuracy. We have not run paired samples against a known ground truth, and we are not claiming CRISPRpreserve is more accurate than CRISPResso2.

Why CRISPRpreserve?

🧬

Every Read Classified

Reads are classified and reported rather than dropped at a homology cutoff. Low confidence alignments are flagged so you can see what the tool was unsure about instead of losing it silently.

⚡

Zero Installation

Upload your FASTQ, paste your reference, click analyze. No conda environments, no Docker containers, no dependency conflicts. Works in any browser.

🎯

Driven By Your Guide, Not The PAM

Quantification is based on the sgRNA coordinates you supply rather than PAM sequence or nuclease identity, so Cas9, Cas12a, Cas12f and other nucleases are handled the same way with no PAM configuration. Indels and substitutions are both called and reported, and base conversions are tallied separately. Prime editing outcomes are not specifically modelled.
